# llama.cpp v1.0.0 : A stronger foundation for the local AI ecosystem

llama.cpp makes it possible to run multi-modal LLMs across a wide range of hardware - from consumer-grade local machines to high-end enterprise servers. Through quantization, optimized backends, and a portable C/C++ implementation, we make AI inference accessible at every level of the software stack. We believe this is the most lightweight, versatile and sensible platform for building cross-platform and private AI products.

During the 3.5 years since itsinception, the project has grown a lot and many downstream applications already depend on our work. We expect this trend to continue as local AI and privacy become increasingly important in our everyday lives. Every new integration means more developers depending on our library interfaces and therefore having a flexible but stable API is paramount.

Today we release librariesv1.0.0, highlighting our responsibility to those developers. Moving forward, bothllama.cppandggmlwill reflect API and ABI incompatibilities with major-version bumps. As adoption grows, we will be stricter about making those boundaries visible to downstream consumers.

### Development

The project has fostered an amazing open-source community with highly-skilled and talented contributors. With the rapid advance of AI capabilities in the field of software engineering, we continue to adapt and integrate LLMs in our developement cycle in a thoughtful and resonable manner. Maintaining a high quality codebase, staying in control of the architecture and understanding the implementation details will remain our top priorities.

To reinforce the faster pace of development we continue to invest more and more resources in improving the CI infrastructure behind the projects. ### Backend and model coverage

The underlying machine learning engine ofllama.cpp-ggmlis designed to run on virtually any hardware in existence. This is achieved by implementing dedicated ggml backends. Each backend can be compiled as a dynamic library and optionally loaded at runtime.

A complete list of the currently implemented backends can be foundhere.

We are committed to provide continuous support of all model architectures. Currently, llama.cpp supports more than150 architecture variantsas well as all mainstream speculative decoding methods.
