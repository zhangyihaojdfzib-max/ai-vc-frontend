In short :Five frontier models now sit within five points of each other in terms of intelligence, but their cost per unit differs by 3.8x. Google, which has built chips alongside its models for more than 15 years, prices lowest; Microsoft & OpenAI are now chasing the same advantage, & pricing should keep deflating even as frontier margins hold.

Five frontier models now sit within five points of each other in terms of intelligence, but their cost per unit differs by 3.8x.

![The AI grocery store aisle: a grouped bar chart of weighted average cost per Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index task for five frontier models, grouped by index score; the three models tied at 53 points span $1.99 to $7.63](/images/posts/ef501cbdf506.jpg)

Google’s Argon prices at the bottom of the cluster at $1.99 a task1, 74% below the most expensive model, Fable 5.1. It is also 67% below Opus 5.5, 56% below Muse Wonder & 39% below Astra.

If we are to believe Michael Mauboussin’s principle that prices convey information2, then we can deduce that different models, architectures & different costs of GPUs translate directly here.

Why could this be?

Google has been building machine learning models in parallel to its chips for well more than 15 years, more experience than anyone else on this list3.

The evidence is in Google’s own peer-reviewed work on its chips, their Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). The fourth iteration TPU chips, released in 2023, ran 1.2x to 1.7x faster than the Nvidia A100 while drawing 1.3x to 1.9x less power4. At the low end of the throughput range against the midpoint of the power range, that yields 80% more tokens for the same serving cost5.

If that advantage has carried through to today, then Google could very well be serving tokens at 44% less than its peers, while operating at similar margins. It would also explain the disproportionate scale of Google’s data center buildout relative to its smaller cloud business6.

Google is no longer alone in this. Every lab that can afford custom silicon is now building it. Microsoft’s Satya Nadella, introducing Maia 300 in October 2026 :

“Starting next calendar year, we will scale our Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments, making it the primary inference system for our own product and services. And we’re introducing Maia 300, which delivers 40% more tokens per watt compared to leading GPU-based systems.”7

OpenAI’s first inference chip, Jalapeño, went further. Tested on SemiAnalysis’s InferenceX suite against Nvidia’s GB200 & GB300, it delivered 1.5x to 1.9x more throughput per watt & 1.7x to 3.6x lower end-to-end latency :

“Jalapeño delivered a better combination of throughput, power efficiency, and latency. Although performance is sometimes reported per chip, we believe the more useful standard is performance per unit of power.”8

As intelligence per watt doubles every six months, pricing should continue to deflate in the world of tokens, with margins remaining in the 40-70% range, particularly for frontier models9.
