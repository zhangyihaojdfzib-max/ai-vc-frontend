In short :NVIDIA's $107.6b Q3 Data Center business faces a strategic dilemma : hyperscalers ($60.1b) are building custom ASICs to replace NVIDIA GPUs, while neoclouds & frontier labs ($47.5b) build no competing chips but need NVIDIA's balance sheet. After peaking at 45.3% in Q2, neocloud share dipped to 44.1% in Q3 as NVIDIA tightened financing terms.

NVIDIA’s hardest strategic problem is not demand : it is choosing which customer to feed.1

Hyperscalers like Google, Amazon, Microsoft & Meta buy $60.1b of NVIDIA hardware each quarter while racing to replace it with their own custom silicon. Neoclouds & frontier labs buy $47.5b & build no competing chips, yet rely on NVIDIA’s balance sheet to scale.

After climbing to a 45% share of Data Center revenue last quarter, non-hyperscaler share ticked down to 44% in Q3 just as NVIDIA signaled tighter financing terms.2

![NVIDIA Data Center revenue doubled to $107.6b as both buyer groups scaled](/images/posts/92c53eca789d.jpg)

Hyperscale revenue grew 71% year over year to $60.1b, while neocloud & enterprise revenue nearly tripled to $47.5b on strong Vera Rubin shipments.2

“AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise revenue of $47 billion increased 18% sequentially & nearly tripled year-over-year.”

Yet NVIDIA is tightening the bar on neocloud financing. It holds $102b in cloud & data center lease commitments, including $32b directly with neoclouds, alongside $390b in purchase commitments.2Interim CEO & CFO Colette Kress laid down a firmer line, demanding operators bring both powered shells & their own capital :

“We have supported the development of this ecosystem, & we will continue to be disciplined & selective in our support.”

Because neoclouds lack hyperscaler cash flows, a more selective backstop could moderate growth for smaller, capital-constrained operators.3

A diversified buyer base is NVIDIA’s best defense against hyperscaler custom silicon. Squeezing neoclouds hands that leverage right back to Google, Amazon, Microsoft & Meta.
