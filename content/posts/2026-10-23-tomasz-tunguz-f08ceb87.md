In short :Every major build-out has two phases per Carlota Perez: infrastructure, then the applications that pay for it. The telecom internet build-out ran capex twelve times ahead of application revenue at the 2000 peak, & took seven years to get application revenue above 10% of capex, & ten to reach 48%. AI has reached that 10% mark in four years, roughly half the time.

Every major technology build-out requires two phases, according to Carlota Perez1: the infrastructure to power it, & then the applications to pay for it.

Between 1996 & 2005, American telecom companies spent $1.5t building the internet in today’s dollars.2Meanwhile, applications riding on that infrastructure booked $180 billion of revenue.3

That’s an interesting ratio, isn’t it? Application revenue to infrastructure spend. It matters because the shorter the gap between the two, the sooner the infrastructure pays for itself. Let’s compare this to the AI era.

It would take a decade for dark fiber to fill its promise, flooded with the packets of application revenue. AI is achieving that mark in half the time.

AI hit double digits in its fourth year. Plus, the unit economics from companies like SpaceX suggest that the paybacks on the infrastructure from this revenue are short, about a year.4

![Grouped bars comparing US venture capital invested by year since each build-out began, web era against AI era, in 2026 dollars](/images/posts/d6f039ed4107.jpg)

US venture invested $516b in the first nine months of 2026. That is already 44% above the full-year record set in 2021.5Inflation-adjusted, the run-up in venture investment is roughly two & a half times the size of the earlier era.6

In a very real sense, the entire ecosystem is hoping the gap between application revenue & infrastructure investment is significantly shorter on an inflation-adjusted basis. Cumulative venture investment in this wave is about six times the first four years of the web era.6

The major risk is whether the ecosystem is an ouroboros: is all the spend just within startups?7

Ramp’s data show that 80% of the largest AI spenders are venture-backed. Those firms now account for more than 13% of American employment, up from about 5% during the web era. The circularity is real, but the circle is a sixth of the economy.

That is a much larger circle.8
