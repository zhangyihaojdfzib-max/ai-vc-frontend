Kazunari Hanaohas joined Vercel as Country Manager for Japan, based in Tokyo. His appointment is part of our continued investment in the Japanese market, including plans to expand our local team.

Japanese developers were early adopters of Next.js. And today, Vercel's customers in Japan include Mitsubishi Corporation, Fujitsu Limited, U-NEXT CO., Ltd., and DeNA Co., Ltd., alongside startups like SANU Inc. and Reiwa Travel, Inc.

Kazunari has spent the last decade building and leading software businesses in Japan. He joins Vercel from Pendo, where he was President of Pendo Japan. Before that, he built HashiCorp’s Japan business and spent nearly five years in sales leadership at Pivotal in Tokyo.

![Kazunari Hanao, Country Manager for Japan at Vercel.](/images/posts/c9353bbc864d.jpg)

I asked him about the opportunity in Japan and his decision to join Vercel.

### Copy link to headingWhy are you optimistic about Vercel's future in Japan?

Japan is at a turning point in how software gets built. The country has innovative companies and talented developers, but complex enterprise IT environments are slowing the adoption of frontier AI to the point that businesses can’t capture its value.

Vercel’s opportunity is to help Japanese enterprises move from experimentation to production, and run AI applications securely, reliably, and efficiently at scale. Cost, governance, and security for AI have become business priorities, not just technical ones. Almost every CXO I've met recently is working through those questions.

### Copy link to headingWhat interested you in working for Vercel?

I've always been drawn to technology that simplifies complexity and helps businesses move faster. Vercel felt like a natural continuation of that, with bigger potential. AI is transforming the whole software development lifecycle, and Vercel has a real chance to become a foundational platform for the next generation of applications.

Ultimately what convinced me was the vision, the developer-first culture, and the chance to build something in Japan from the ground up. I enjoy building businesses, bringing talented people together, and creating an environment where teams feel empowered to take on hard problems. Vercel gives me the chance to do that with technology I believe in.

### Copy link to headingWhat have you learned building businesses in Japan for global companies?

Building a successful business is about much more than growing revenue. It starts with bringing together the right people, uniting them around a shared goal, earning the trust of customers and partners, and building a culture where every team member feels ownership.

I've also learned how much it matters to connect global innovation with the specific needs of Japanese customers. During our first meetings, Guillermo and I talked about a future where countless digital services in Japan run on Vercel, and people experience its value every day whether they realize it or not. "Vercel Everywhere" is the future I want to build.
