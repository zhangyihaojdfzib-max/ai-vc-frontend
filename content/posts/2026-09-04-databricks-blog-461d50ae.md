## 2. Marketing Funnel Attribution

The problem:Marketing teams are regularly asked which programs contributed to pipeline and revenue, but the answer depends on the attribution approach the team has chosen. An email nurture may help move an existing prospect forward, while a paid-search campaign or event may be associated with an opportunity’s creation. The same opportunity can receive different credit under first-touch, sourced, or multi-touch attribution, so teams need to use the same definitions when they discuss performance. Marketing analytics teams will often set up dashboards to capture overall funnel performance and allow for cohort tracking, but dashboards alone can’t answer the nuanced questions that campaign and channel owners need to answer to plan and evaluate their strategies.

How Genie One helps:Marketers can ask Genie One complex funnel questions in plain language, such as:“Which campaigns influenced the most pipeline this quarter?”or“Where did MQL-to-opportunity conversion decline by business unit?”This lets you explore a number or trend beyond a dashboard’s bounds without having to locate underlying tables, translate the question into filters, or build a separate report.

When a team uses the same question repeatedly, it can be saved as a reusable skill or configured as a shareable Genie Agent for that workflow. Campaign managers and regional teams can then start from the same question, definitions, and attribution method for every inquiry, without rebuilding your prompt. Simply share the skill instructions orGenie Agentwith the people involved in the review, and schedule the recurring analysis or dashboard update for the team’s regular operating cadence.

## 3. 360 Customer Insights

The problem:Customer dashboards can show metrics such as product adoption, engagement, account tier, renewal timing, and pipeline. But when a marketer sees a change, such as lower usage among a particular customer segment, the dashboard might not explain which accounts are driving it, what those accounts have in common, or whether the team should respond. Answering those follow-up questions often requires new analysis or help from a data team.

How Genie One helps:Start with the customer dashboard to identify a change worth investigating, then use Genie One to explore it further. For example, you could ask,“Which enterprise accounts have seen product usage decline in the last 30 days?”Genie One can identify the relevant accounts and surface related signals in the organization’s data, such as recent engagement, adoption of key features, renewal timing, or account segment.

The analysis can be tailored to the person using it. A lifecycle marketer can ask to see customers that are eligible for an adoption campaign, while an account-based marketer may ask for a prioritized list of named accounts and the signals behind that prioritization. Based on the patterns it finds, Genie One can also help identify follow-up areas to review, such as feature adoption, recent engagement, renewal timing, or whether an account is already engaged in an active customer success motion. Those recommendations give each team a clearer place to start, while marketers retain responsibility for the decision and the campaign plan.

Teams can share the conversation as a record of the analysis, or turn it into a ready-to-share document complete with visuals right from Genie One. You can then schedule the analysis as a recurring task on a weekly, monthly, or quarterly basis to ensure proactive recommendations are ready for you on your regular planning cycle.

## 5. Business Reporting On-the-Go

The problem:Marketing leaders often need to check a number or resolve a question between meetings, before a customer conversation, or while preparing for a leadership review. The issue is not simply finding a report; it’s understanding what changed and whether it needs attention. A pipeline figure may be below plan, a launch could pace unevenly by region, or a campaign might generate responses without producing qualified opportunities.

A general-purpose AI assistant can summarize the information included in a prompt, but it doesn’t know which pipeline definition, attribution method, fiscal period, or source of truth the marketing organization uses. As a result, leaders may still need to provide that context, compare the response with other systems, or ask a team member to confirm it.

How Genie One helps:A leader can open theGenie One mobile app, then ask a focused follow-up question such as,“What changed in sourced pipeline this week?”or“Which regions are behind launch pace?”Genie One can connect the reported change to the accounts, campaigns, or regions behind it, giving the leader enough context to decide whether a follow-up is needed.

The mobile app supports questions in natural language, interactive dashboards, and follow-up analysis within a dashboard. It can also run scheduled tasks, so a leader can set up a regular summary, such as a Monday pipeline-pacing update or a pre-meeting launch-status review.
