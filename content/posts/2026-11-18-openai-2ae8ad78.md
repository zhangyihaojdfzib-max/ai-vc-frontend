The program aims to support up to 25,000 young people over the next 18 months, helping them develop practical skills and confidence to move toward education and employment in an AI-enabled economy.

The program will help address a gap in young people’s experience of AI while responding to their appetite to learn. The Open University’sBusiness Barometer 2026⁠(opens in a new window)found that 68% of 16–24-year-olds surveyed who were not in education, employment or training would be willing to train or upskill to improve their job prospects.

It forms part of a wider program of nationally recognized AI qualifications being developed by The King’s Trust, designed to equip young people with the skills employers need as AI transforms the world of work.

Working with employers, AI specialists and young people, The King’s Trust is exploring the skills young people will need in a world of work where AI is becoming part of everyday life, and how these can complement the employability skills already embedded across its programs.

Building on a 50-year legacy of supporting more than 1.3 million young people, The King’s Trust wants to ensure the next generation gains the skills and confidence needed to thrive in an AI-enabled economy.
