# ChatGPT Plus for veterans and servicemembers

## 20,000 veterans are using ChatGPT to build what comes next

In the past year, more than 20,000 transitioning servicemembers and veterans have claimed a year ofChatGPT Plus at no costafter being offered free access by OpenAI. That milestone gives us a chance to look at how veterans are using AI to prepare for civilian careers and to figure out what comes next for themselves and their families.

The effort started with veterans who work here at OpenAI, people who use these tools every day and know firsthand how challenging the transition can be. Beyond the free access, OpenAI Academy and our community partners have hosted more than a dozen trainings for nearly 1,000 members of the military community,with seven more⁠(opens in a new window)scheduled before the year ends.

## Preparing for civilian careers

Leaving the military often means stepping into an unfamiliar job market and working out how years of service translate into a civilian role. Many veterans are turning to ChatGPT to organize their job search, rehearse for interviews, and explore paths they hadn’t considered.

Air Force veteranTilak Bhatnagar⁠(opens in a new window)used his free year to prepare for a dozen interviews, find the right words to describe his military experience to civilian employers, and weigh competing job offers. Along the way, those conversations helped him clarify what he actually wanted from life after active duty.

He ultimately took a role at a tech company. What he valued most was being able to pick up where he left off, returning to the same thread about his background, goals, and constraints. As he put it, “each conversation built on the last.”

Sadarian Bell⁠(opens in a new window), also an Air Force veteran, started planning more than a year before he left the service. He used ChatGPT to map out his transition and to turn nearly a decade of military healthcare experience into a civilian résumé.

To prepare for interviews, he practiced out loud using ChatGPT voice mode, working through one question at a time until his answers felt natural. “It has genuinely become my career coach,” Sadarian said, though he also learned to recognize when he’d had enough help and could trust his own judgment.

For Navy veteranKelli Ramirez⁠(opens in a new window), the transition was a chance to chase a long-held dream: becoming a published author. She’d already invested in a book-writing course to sharpen her craft, and ChatGPT filled in the gaps. “ChatGPT helped me organize ideas, ask questions about publishing, and work through the Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing process,” she said.

Kelli has since published two books, which she says “reflect my desire to help women approach technology with confidence and, for those who share my Christian faith, with wisdom grounded in that faith.”

## Learning to use ChatGPT, together

Not everyone arrives confident about using AI tools, which is why OpenAI Academy gives veterans, transitioning servicemembers, and military spouses a place to learn alongside people who understand what they’re going through.

OpenAI recently wrapped our first six-event Academy series withVeterans Forge⁠(opens in a new window), focused on helping veterans and military families use ChatGPT to work toward their goals. That pilot, together with workshops alongside AI Collective Hampton Roads and NPower, is shaping what we offer veterans next.

At oneworkshop in Reston, Virginia⁠(opens in a new window), nearly100 veterans and active-duty servicemembers⁠(opens in a new window)explored ways to put AI to work. ParticipantRyan Kaono-Wedemeyer⁠(opens in a new window)learned to start by writing out a detailed account of his military experience, then asking AI to help shape it into a civilian résumé.

InstructorErin Marie⁠(opens in a new window)helped participants build custom GPTs to support their transitions, and watched people who walked in skeptical begin finding ways the tools could genuinely help them.

In Louisville, Kentucky,more than 80 Legionnaires⁠(opens in a new window)attended three AI training sessions at the American Legion’s national convention. For those who can’t make it to an event, ouronline veterans collection⁠(opens in a new window)and100 chats for veterans⁠(opens in a new window)offer examples to try on your own.
