# How Steven Basart forced his way into Beacon Health and became its first engineer

![](/images/posts/61e4796f5414.jpg)

Before Steven Basart joined Beacon Health, he co-founded his own nonprofit and got accepted into a YC batch. He dropped out, but along the way he met Beacon Health's cofounders, Mark and Obinna. "I basically forced my way in to work with them because they were cool and could crush," he says. He weighed his other offers, decided he could make a bigger impact here, and became the first or second hired employee at the company.

Beacon Health (W26) automates back office work at hospitals and practices and tries to, as Steven puts it, "make EHRs not suck or at least suck less."

## Nice cofounders, real mission

Ask Steven what makes Beacon Health a good place to work and the answer comes fast: the people. "Honestly these are some of the nicest cofounders you'll ever meet," he says. He once made a mistake that cost the company $20,000. Instead of blowback, the founders picked him back up, negotiated the payment, and moved on. "Things are super friendly and we can disagree but it's all okay because it's all for the sake of the company."

The mission matters too. Making people actually healthier is a straightforward goal that holds up on hard days.

![](/images/posts/d8c2d536ea01.jpg)

## What he's actually built

Steven's official title is Head of Engineering, but he describes the real job as "get whatever stuff done." The list of things he's owned is wide:

- An EHR exporter built on top of FHIR
- Getting the company to compliance
- A pipeline to analyze patient documents and reconcile errors — "AI all the way down"
- A mini internal programming language called Iris that lets customers write basic rules that compile into executable logic

"The more I do and accomplish the more they let me do and continue to operate and build," he says. Iris is the project he's proudest of — a small, scrappy language that gives non-technical users real power without requiring them to write production code.

## No leash, lots of trust

Steven says working with the founders feels less like being managed and more like being consulted. "There's basically no leash and it's almost the other way around. They come to me sometimes for advice. How would you do things." His role has stayed deliberately flexible. He expects it to sharpen into something more fixed only once the company grows enough to need that.

On the tools side, he keeps it simple: "I am a basic Claude Code monkey. If Claude isn't integrated I'll just build an integration with Claude so it can get integrated."

![](/images/posts/c1a60111da7e.jpg)

## Who belongs here

Steven sees founding-stage work as messy by design — "doing whatever to keep the company operating and running and getting to more money." He isn't sure the startup path is for everyone, but he's clear about who thrives in it: "If you're good at taking ownership and working under uncertainty then it's one of the best experiences you could ever do. Learn your coworkers and what you like and don't like. Things move fast so you can learn fast too."

If you want to build across the entire stack at a healthcare company where the founders trust you to figure it out, Beacon Health ishiring.
