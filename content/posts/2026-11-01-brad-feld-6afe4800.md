You can check your own box. Just not yet.

A few days ago, I wroteLife Imitates Art: The $6.3 Billion Binance Hackand ended it with “I wonder what is going to happen next week.” It’s Monday morning in New Zealand, and a bunch has already happened.

Binance said it would reopen withdrawals in phases on Sunday, and it did. Bitcoin went first, between 06:00 and 09:00 UTC, followed by 14 other networks, including Ethereum, Solana, and the big stablecoins. CZ’s announcement was four words: “BTC withdrawal is live.”

Customers then did what customers do. By Sunday afternoon, Binance had processed$4.6 billion in withdrawals and $1 billion in deposits, a net outflow of about $3.6 billion, or 2.1% of its $171 billion in assets. (Last week, CNBC said $145 billion. I’ve stopped trying to reconcile crypto numbers.) Binance says the flows are “stabilising.” Inchapter 54ofZero Knowledge, a credit union IT person spends a Friday calling 63 banks trying to get 412 paychecks accepted. By comparison, a 2% outflow isn’t a run. It’s a jog.

Binancesaysit has now covered 95% of the stolen money, up from 80% on Friday and 90% on Saturday. The remaining 5% is less-liquid tokens it’s still buying. The money came from part of SAFU, part of its futures insurance fund, and its corporate treasury, and Binance says it will refill SAFU to about $1 billion. It also promised a post-incident report once the investigation is complete.

The FBI hasofficially attributedthe hack to North Korea.

Then there’s the proof of reserves, which co-CEO Richard Teng promised by Monday. Binance posted a new reserves snapshot on Saturday, but itslatest updatesays the full proof will be ready “by Tuesday, 2026-11-03 (UTC),” because it takes 40 to 50 hours of computation to generate. The proof is supposed to let each customer check that their balance was included in what Binance owes, without seeing anyone else’s.

It’s a zero-knowledge proof which, well, made me giggle with delight since my book is namedZero Knowledge. If you want to understand the math (and what happens when it breaks), the book’s appendix,How It Actually Works, covers it.
