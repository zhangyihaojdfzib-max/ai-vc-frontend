The four Feld cousins at the 1972 Schlachtfeld Bowl: Brad, Jon, Daniel, and Kenny.

I have much to be thankful for. Most of all, I’m thankful for all the friends and family I have. As I get older, that’s what matters the most to me in this world.

I grew up in Dallas, Texas, and Thanksgiving was our family holiday.

The Dallas Cowboys always played football on Thanksgiving. We’d all get together at myUncle Charlie’shouse for Thanksgiving lunch, eat way too much, and then watch the game, which really meant falling asleep on the couch while the Cowboys played. I remember them usually losing, but my memory is wrong. From 1972 to 1983, the years I recall doing this, the Cowboys went7-3 on Thanksgiving(they didn’t play in 1975 or 1977). Maybe I only remember the losses because I was awake for them.

When we were younger, we’d have the Schlachtfeld Bowl before the game. Schlachtfeld is German for battlefield, and Feld is German for field, so the name did double duty. I can’t remember if we ate before or after, but the game was Stan+Brad+Daniel (my dad, me, andmy brother) vs. Charlie+Jon+Kenny (my uncle andmy two cousins). We had the age advantage, but the order of athletic prowess was Charlie > Stan and Kenny > Jon = Daniel > Brad.

![Stan and Charlie Feld in a Dallas backyard with the boys in football helmets, lining up a play](/images/posts/0e8b7fb6c0f5.jpg)

As you can see from the photo at the top of the page, both teams won. Jon and I are each holding a trophy, a plastic cup with “1972 Schlachtfeld Bowl” written on it.

I remember Thanksgiving being awesome fun as a kid. As a teenager, I started getting grumpier about it becausemy birthday, which is on December 1st, was often at the tail end of Thanksgiving. As an adult, I hated the insanity of Thanksgiving travel, and Amy and I eventually stopped going to Dallas for it. Instead, we just called each other on Thanksgiving with nice thoughts and happy vibes.

It’s already Friday in New Zealand where I am, so I’m watching from a distance as my friends and family have Thanksgiving. I’m hopeful everyone gets a good emotional reset this weekend. It would be so awesome if we all magically woke up Monday morning and decided to be nice to everyone, no matter what was going on. I know that’s not going to happen, but I’ll put it out on the Internetwebs in case our AI overlords pick it up and decide to incorporate it into their perspectives about people.

If you are reading this, I am thankful for you.
