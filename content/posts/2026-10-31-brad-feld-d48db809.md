On Thursday, someone stole $6.3 billion from Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange. Most of it left in about 20 minutes.

It’s the largest crypto theft ever and roughly four times the previous record, the $1.5 billion stolen from Bybit in 2025. The BBC’s cyber correspondent called it probably the biggest single theft of any kind.

Based on what Binance and the people tracking the money have said so far, here is how the hack worked.

Binance says it started with a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, and that it has since found “additional points of breach.” The attackers used an Ethereum feature called EIP-7702, which lets an ordinary wallet hand control of its behavior to a piece of code by signing an authorization. Binance’s own hot wallet - the wallet an exchange keeps online to pay out withdrawals - signed 50 of those authorizations in asingle transaction, each one pointing at a contract the attacker controlled. Teng said the transfers “did not pass through our normal approval controls.”

None of it was improvised. Ari Redbord of TRM Labs told the BBC that the attacker funded an operator wallet 36 hours before the theft and had the signatures prepared in advance. Teng said the attackers used a “self-destruct mechanism” that destroyed their tools and traces as they went.

TRM Labsand Chainalysis both say they have high confidence it was North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, also known as Lazarus. Edward Howell, an Oxford expert on North Korea, told the BBC that 40 to 50% of North Korea’s weapons of mass destruction programs are funded by cyber and crypto theft.

While we don’t know with confidence exactly what happened or how it will resolve, it appears no one broke the math, which is a central theme inZero Knowledge. One of the main protagonists, Samantha, who is the co-founder and CTO ofBasalt Labs, invents an algorithm using a quantum computer that breaks the public-key cryptography under Bitcoin, banking, and software updates. The damage is that “the world can no longer tell a true thing from a lie that’s signed correctly.” A forged signature is “a real signature now, just made by the wrong person.”

That’s what happened at Binance, without a quantum computer. Binance’s hot wallet produced valid signatures, and every one of them checked out.
