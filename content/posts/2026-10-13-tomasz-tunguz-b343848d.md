In short :Theory's second annual AI in Practice Survey finds four results that surprised us : 43% rate security a top pain against the 10% who say its tooling delivers value, 45% of teams route queries across models, Cloudflare hosts 62% of agent sandboxes, & 48% now read production logs with coding agents.

The 2026 Theory AI Survey surprised us, just as the 2025 edition did. In 2025, we found that context, limited AI performance. In 2026, that seems to be solved.

455 senior technical builders replied to give us a cross-section of the AI ecosystem today.1

## Security is the biggest barrier to adoption

Security rose from sixth to first among pain points in twelve months. 43% of respondents rate security & governance the most important pain point, & evals come in second at less than half, 22%.

Only 10% say security tooling delivers the most value today. 18% expect it to lead in the future, the largest expected gain of any area.

![Animated chart of seven tooling areas by pain, each with a line from value delivered today to value expected in the future; security and governance reports 43% pain against 10% value today and 18% expected](/images/posts/d452d11baab8.gif)

## Nearly half of teams route queries

Cost optimisation as well as model selection have become standard.

45% of teams now route queries across models, 23.7% on a router they trained & 21.5% on a proprietary one, while 54.7% send every query to a single model.

![Ranked bar chart of routing practice : 54.7% send all queries to one model, 23.7% trained a custom router, 21.5% use a proprietary router](/images/posts/385c3929a580.jpg)

## Cloudflare dominates sandboxes

Of the 259 teams writing code with agents, 62% host their sandboxes on Cloudflare, three times the closest competitor. Then Other at 17%, Vercel at 17%, Modal at 16%, E2B at 12%, Daytona at 9% & Sail at 7%. The one control a builder cannot fake is concentrated in a single utility, & it is billed as compute rather than as security.

![Ranked bar chart of sandbox hosting : Cloudflare 62.2%, Other 17.4%, Vercel 16.6%, Modal 15.8%, E2B 12.4%, Daytona 8.5%, Sail 6.6%](/images/posts/373423b7dfea.jpg)

## BI performs more than half of agent analysis

56% of respondents use business intelligence tools to analyse the performance of their agents. The surprising statistic is not far behind : coding agents also perform some of the work, at 48%. Both systems are excellent at parsing agent behaviour. A roughly equivalent number use dedicated observability tools, at 41%, which does suggest a convergence of the category, at least for this one use case. Spreadsheets, that tardigrade2of enterprise software, continue to survive at 30%.

![Ranked bar chart of how teams review AI interaction data : traditional BI or data science 55.8%, logs with coding agents 48.1%, LLM observability 41.1%, spreadsheets 28.4%, no review 7.0%](/images/posts/c7cf8b22f555.jpg)

There is a lot more to unpack within thefull survey, including open versus closed source model adoption, the rise of MCP despite doubts last year, & the still early adoption of evals. Overall, it is clear how quickly the ecosystem is evolving, & how much sophistication has permeated the enterprise, more quickly than any wave we have seen.
