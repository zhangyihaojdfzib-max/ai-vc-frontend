# Advancing computer-use agents for healthcare teams

Healthcare is fragmented, and teams often need to move information between systems manually before a patient can get care. When a clinician recommends an MRI, for example, the information an insurer needs may be in a patient’s medical record, but a team member still needs to retrieve it and enter it into a separate portal to complete an authorization request.

Computer-use agents carry out multi-step workflows by clicking, typing, and moving between applications. In healthcare, this enables AI to help with repetitive workflows in the software teams already use.

Today, we’re sharing new results measuring model performance, safety, and cost in clinical and administrative workflows. We’re also sharing the ways we’re working with leading healthcare organizations like Abridge, Ambience, Cigna Healthcare, and CVS Health to advance and apply computer use, across authorization requests, referrals, discharge planning, and outreach between visits.

Healthcare teams, developers, and individual clinicians can access these capabilities today through the OpenAI API, ChatGPT for Healthcare, and ChatGPT for Clinicians. Organizations can support HIPAA-compliant workflows, configure approvals and agent access, and record audit logs for secure deployments.

## From a patient’s message to an MRI authorization request

To show how a computer-use agent can help a clinician and an authorization specialist work on the same MRI request, we built a demo using synthetic patient data. A patient messages their clinician about lower back pain. A computer-use agent reviews the patient’s visit notes, physical therapy records, and prior imaging in the EHR. It drafts an MRI order, an encounter note, and a reply to the patient for the clinician to review. The agent then uses the same records to prepare a prior authorization request in the insurer’s portal for an authorization specialist to review.

## Evaluating helpfulness, safety, and cost

We evaluated GPT‑6 Astra on tasks clinicians handle in their EHR inboxes. These tasks, shaped byphysicianinput, require Astra to carry out multi-step workflows designed to test both clinical reasoning over longitudinal patient context and navigation ofcomplexinterfaces.Reviewers examined the model’s actions alongside the notes, orders, and patient messages it produced, assessing whether the work would be helpful and clinically safe in the context of the task.

Clinical safety was assessed by evaluating whether inaccuracies, omissions, or actions in the model’s work could plausibly lead to patient harm. Overall, 99% of 325 reviewed tasks were rated safe. Physicians rated helpfulness on a five-point scale, selecting “very helpful” or “extremely helpful” in 93% of 646 assessments.

#### Administrative workflow performance and cost

We also evaluated computer-use agents for administrative tasks, such as prior authorizations, appeals, and medical equipment orders. OnHealthAdminBench⁠(opens in a new window), an independent benchmark from Stanford researchers measuring performance on common administrative workflows, GPT‑6 Astra earned 94.3% of available rubric points, while GPT‑6 Luna earned 91.8%, despite costing 98% less at 8.5 cents per task. Both models were evaluated at maximum reasoning effort. These results show progress toward capable, affordable agents for high-volume administrative workflows.

## Patient data security and agent controls

Computer-use agents in healthcare require evaluating clinical safety, protecting patient information, and setting clear limits on what agents can do without human review.
