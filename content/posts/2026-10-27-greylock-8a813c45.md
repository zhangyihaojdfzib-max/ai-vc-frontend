## Of the many transformative possibilities of artificial intelligence, its potential to impact education is among the most exciting.

It’s also one of the most contentious.

AI could be used to extend the reach of overworked teachers, develop personalized curriculums that account for students’ different learning styles, and introduce engaging new methods of interactive teaching. But it could also contribute to existing issues within the education system and even introduce a host of new problems to grapple with. Like many new tools, it’s possible that AI would first be used in schools already ahead of the curve in terms of available resources to purchase and implement the technology, further widening the gap between affluent and under-privileged students.

Furthermore, it could breed reliance on technology that impedes the development of critical thinking skills, enables cheating, and effectively pushes human teachers out of the role.

While concerns about AI’s abuse and misuse are valid, the possibilities to enhance and improve the learning experience at every level of education cannot be overlooked. Like any other application of AI, humans have the most important role to play in devising guardrails and practices for the safe deployment of the technology.

In fact, ChatGPT, the large language model created byOpenAI(where I serve on the Board of Directors) had some interesting ideas for how this could be done. We dove into the topic of AI in education for our most recent installment of Fireside Chatbots, a special podcast miniseries running as part of Greymatter’sIntelligent Futureseries on artificial intelligence. Throughout this series, I have been discussing various aspects of AIwithAI, including a look back at theorigins of AI and its subsequent evolution, and thehuman-AI partnership in creative endeavorslike writing and art.
