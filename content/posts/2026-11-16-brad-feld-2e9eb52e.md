My mom read my post aboutThe Bookshop of Forbidden Booksand sent me a note. It included this:

I do remember you starting to read at 3. We used flash cards! That was the first-grade teacher in me.

She told me that when she was growing up in the 1940s, her library had a locked cabinet behind the librarian’s desk. You could check those books out, but only with an adult. She thinks they were mostly about the human body. That was the closest thing to a banned book she knew about, and an adult with a library card could still open the cabinet.

I wrote back that she and my dad gave me one of the best gifts a kid can get. They encouraged me to read but never forced it. They read with me, so I felt like a grownup. They bought me books constantly, took me to bookstores, and let me pick out whatever I wanted. They dropped me off at the Richardson Library and the Fretz Park Library and let me stay there alone for hours. I still have a recurring library dream, and it’s a happy one.

They let me pull anything off their shelves, which is how a ten-year-old ends up readingFahrenheit 451andFuture Shock. They let me read in the back of the car on the way to things I wasn’t interested in, on camping trips in the tent, on boats (I hated being on boats so I hid in a book), during the things I wasn’t interested in, and at night when everyone else was watching TV.

Mom, Dad, thank you for the wonderful gift of reading and books. And being awesome parents. I count myself very lucky.

Amy and I have a call and response we’ve been doing for years. It doesn’t matter who says which line.

“What do you want to do tonight?”

“How about going dancing?”

“Nah, let’s read instead.”

Lying on the couch at night reading is a core part of my relationship with Amy.

According toGoodreads, I’ve read 2,167 books. I joined Goodreads in December 2011, so the flash card years aren’t in there. Neither is the Bronx, where I read more than just James Michener’sThe Drifterson visits to my grandparents, or the back seat of the car.

My currently-reading shelf has one book on it:Alfaby Lilja Sigurðardóttir. It won’t be there long.

I’ve been writing about books on this blog since 2004. I went back through the archives and picked one book from each year. Not the best book of the year, just one whose post made me smile when I reread it.

- 2004 -The Kite Runnerby Khaled Hosseini.Amy finished it first, called me on my cell phone, and told me it was the best first novel she’d read that year. She was right. This turns out to be a pattern.
- 2005 -On Bullshitby Harry Frankfurt.It’s a delightful half-book. It was the second book with “bullshit” in the title I’d read in twelve months, which says something about me or about 2005.
- 2006 -A Dirty Jobby Christopher Moore.I finished it at 2am in an apartment in Paris. The next night, Amy and I went looking for a Mexican restaurant that turned out to have vanished and been replaced by an Indian one.
- 2007 -The Yiddish Policemen’s Unionby Michael Chabon.Amy loved reading it. I loved listening to it on a run through the mountains.
- 2008 -God Bless You, Mr. Rosewaterby Kurt Vonnegut.When Vonnegut died in 2007, I bought 14 of his novels at once and decided to read them all.
- 2009 -Daemonby Daniel Suarez.I wrote “If you read one book in 2009, read Daemon” in January, after reading four books that year. That was bold.
- 2010 -Future Shockby Alvin Toffler.I reread it 40 years after it came out. The first time, I was about ten and had pulled it off my parents’ shelf.
- 2011 -Boomerangby Michael Lewis.It kept me up well past my bedtime. Fifteen years later,Blockersdid the same thing.
- 2012 -Ender’s Gameby Orson Scott Card.I gobbled it down while trying to shake a holiday cold.
- 2013 -Mr. Penumbra’s 24-Hour Bookstoreby Robin Sloan.I read it in two hours, on the couch, during a digital sabbath. Mom, you read it too and don’t remember it, so it’s time to get it on Libby.
- 2014 -The Martianby Andy Weir.At 2am, I said out loud to Amy, who was fast asleep, “I’m going to be tired tomorrow afternoon.”
- 2015 -Sumby David Eagleman.Amy tore a New Yorker article about Eagleman out of the magazine and put it on my to-read pile. That’s how a lot of books get into my life.
- 2016 -Shoe Dogby Phil Knight.It’s the best memoir by a business person I’ve ever read. I consumed it in a day.
- 2017 -Why We Sleepby Matthew Walker.This is part of why Amy and I go to bed between 9 and 10.
- 2018 -A Gentleman in Moscowby Amor Towles.The post starts with Amy, reading on the couch across the room, asking, “How did your book end?” I answered, “Perfectly.” Mom, you just started Towles’The Lincoln Highway, so I’m moving it up my list.
- 2019 -Machines Like Meby Ian McEwan.Amy said, “Wow, you’ll love Ian McEwan’s writing.” Whenever Amy says something like that, I know I’m in for a treat.
- 2020 -The End of Octoberby Lawrence Wright.It’s a pandemic novel, and I read it in May 2020, during a pandemic. Then I took a nap.
- 2021 -The Soul of an Octopusby Sy Montgomery.My favorite animal is still the polar bear, but the octopus gained a lot of ground.
- 2022 -Three Laws Lethalby David Walton.I wrote about Walton’s books in 2015 and promptly forgot about him. Seven years later, he emailed me. I’m glad he did.
- 2023 -Draft No. 4by John McPhee.McPhee is a master of his craft, and this is him writing about it. I didn’t know at the time that I was taking notes for later.
- 2024 -EPOCHby Dave Jilk.My first business partner described the idea to me on a hike around Green Mountain four years before he finished it.
- 2025 -Carrie Soto Is Backby Taylor Jenkins Reid.I read it on the couch between a long nap and a lot of text messages. Our default TV channel is the Tennis Channel, which tells you who controls the remote.
- 2026 -Playgroundby Richard Powers.I finished it around one in the morning, New Zealand time.

I’ve writtennine books. They’re all non-fiction, and almost all of them are about startups, venture capital, and entrepreneurship. One is about Nietzsche (and entrepreneurship). One I wrote with Amy, and we’re still married after working on it together, which is an accomplishment we are both proud of.

After reading many novels, I’m finally writing one. It’s calledZero Knowledge, a near-term sci-fi quantum-crypto thriller about three MIT grads in Basalt, Colorado, who break the cryptography the world runs on. I’m writing it in public, one chapter at a time, atzeroknowledge.ink. Right now it’s free, with no login and no paywall.

Now that I’m writing fiction, I’m reading even more of it. I’m paying attention to how other writers build characters, describe places, and end chapters.

Amy, what do you want to do tonight?
