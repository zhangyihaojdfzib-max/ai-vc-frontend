Yash Patil’s 18-month-old company Applied Compute just agreed to be acquired by Palo Alto Networks for $4.5B in an all-stock deal. Patil is only 23 — he dropped out of Stanford his sophomore year to work at OpenAI. His coworkers threw him his 21st birthday party.

Beneath the flashy headlines of all the twenty-somethings raising massive rounds, we were curious what it’s actually like to start an AI company so young: the advantages, the expectations, the emotional toll.

We spoke with Patil and two other AI founders who started their companies in their early twenties — Max Brodeur-Urbas of Gumloop and Adit Abraham of Reducto — who shared an honest account of what it’s like being a young founder right now:

- How they wield their youth in enterprise sales, wearing hoodies and Birkenstocks to show they don’t care about the old way of doing things
- Why the closer to the roots of AI you go, the less experience matters
- The paranoia of ever-expanding competition
- Why they’d never mandate 9-9-6 for their employees — but typically clock those hours (or more)

Above all, they seem to feel an unshakeable urgency because this window of opportunity is closing. “My worst fear is looking back at this time with regret for not taking advantage of the opportunity in front of us,” says Max Brodeur-Urbas.
