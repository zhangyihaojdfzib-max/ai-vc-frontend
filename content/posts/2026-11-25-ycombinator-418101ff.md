# How Daniel Zeltser cold-emailed his way to employee #2 at Beacon Health

![](/images/posts/9076495acb76.jpg)

Daniel Zeltser was a student at Duke University when he cold-emailed the CEO of a tiny healthcare startup. Then he followed up. Then he followed up again — enough times that Mark, the CEO, finally answered. "He said he had ten minutes for a call; we talked for thirty." They agreed on a two-week trial. About a week in, Obinna, the CTO, told him he was staying. "So apparently my first contribution was being very difficult to ignore."

That startup wasBeacon Health(W26), a YC startup that builds AI agents to take repetitive work off medical teams' plates — sorting incoming documents, updating patient records, and making sure care doesn't fall through the cracks. Daniel joined as employee #2.

## Why he took the leap

What pulled Daniel away from school wasn't a pitch deck. It was the people and the problem. "The idea that something I built could help a medical team take better care of its patients was pretty hard to pass up," he says. "I wanted to find out what I could do with that kind of responsibility."

![](/images/posts/ed606e859ebe.jpg)

He evaluated Beacon Health the way you'd evaluate a team, not a company. During his trial, he watched how the founders treated each other, how seriously they took the practices they worked with, and how much they enjoyed the problem. "That told me a lot more than a pitch could."

## What he actually builds

Daniel owns the full product engineering loop: get on a call with a medical practice, understand what's eating up their day, build the workflow, and make sure it works for them. His title is founding engineer, but he'd call the real job "whatever gets us unstuck today."

He built a workflow that checks patient charts against a care-gap portal, finds care that's already happened but hasn't been recorded, and updates it with supporting evidence — so practices get credit, and get paid, for keeping patients healthy. "One practice had nine months of reporting to catch up on, and we got it done in a day. Hearing how relieved they were was easily the coolest part."

Another project turned into building an automation runner for a desktop medical-records system, which other engineers started extending. "I wasn't expecting 'teach a Windows app to file faxes' to become such a big part of my life, but here we are."

## The culture behind the startup

Ask Daniel what makes Beacon Health a great place to be and the answer comes fast: "The people, easily." He describes the founders bringing the team together over Uyghur, Guyanese, and Jamaican food after Mark returned from traveling. "When you're working with people who feel like friends, you can ask the dumb question, pitch the weird idea, and put everything into it without worrying about being judged."

He gets real trust from the founders. He can disagree with an approach, propose building something from scratch, and they'll hear him out. "I get to own the work, with people in my corner who really want me to succeed."

![](/images/posts/edef7750e013.jpg)

The biggest surprise about startup life? "How kind it can be. I had this picture of startup founders as permanently intense people who barely slept and expected everyone else to keep up. Mark and Obinna definitely work an absurd amount, but they also have so much humility and care for the people around them. I hadn't realized how much those things could coexist."

## Who would thrive here

Daniel's advice for anyone considering an early startup role is simple: "Find a problem you care about and people you feel comfortable being yourself around. Getting to build something with your friends is pretty hard to beat."

If you want to own the whole problem — from a customer call to a shipped workflow — and you care about making healthcare work better for the people delivering it,Beacon Health is hiring.
