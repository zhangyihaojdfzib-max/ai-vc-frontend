A finance team’s job is not simply to report the numbers: it’s to interpret what they mean for the business, and help CFOs, business partners, and other leaders decide what to do next. Finance teams need to turn data into a trusted decision-ready account of what changed, why it changed, and what to do next.

The challenge is almost never data access. To answer a follow-up question, teams often manually extract data, negotiate competing metric definitions across teams, and assemble the underlying business context. This triggers a slow, cross-functional fact-checking cycle. Ultimately, by the time the answer is ready, the leadership discussion has already passed.

Genie Oneis a data-smart AI coworker grounded in the business context that gives financial data its meaning: approved metrics, fiscal logic, entity structures, and the organization’s own terminology. It helps finance teams investigate the drivers behind a result and turn recurring analysis into reusable workflows, without replacing financial judgment or existing controls.

Here are common finance workflows where teams can begin.

## Budget variance analysis

The problem:A variance report can tell finance that revenue beat plan in EMEA. The harder question is why, and whether the forecast or operating plan needs to change. Answering that usually means untangling performance across business units and entities, then accounting for allocation logic, timing differences, and one-time items.

How Genie One helps:A finance business partner can ask,“Which business units drove the revenue variance in EMEA last quarter?”or“Show the five largest expense lines where actuals exceeded plan by more than 15%.”Genie One understands the company’s approved definitions for measures such as EBITDA and CapEx, along with its fiscal periods and entity rollups, so the analysis starts from the same logic the team already uses and supports a faster, more consistent review.

The finance business partner can review the tables and definitions behind the answer before sharing it with cost-center owners, then schedule the same review for the next monthly or quarterly cycle. For example, at the beauty company Coty,Genie gives finance leaders self-service accessto governed P&L, net revenue, and CapEx data, allowing them to investigate KPI changes as they arise instead of waiting a day for follow-up analysis.

## Cash flow forecasting

The problem:Financial Planning & Analysis (FP&A) and treasury teams need a current view of cash and working-capital drivers, but the inputs, such as receivables, payment terms, open purchase orders, inventory positions, bank activity, and business-unit forecasts, might refresh on different schedules. As a result, while the team may know the projected cash balance, they can’t quickly explain what changed and the contributing factors.

How Genie One helps:Genie One lets teams investigate cash and working-capital questions in plain language:“What is our cash conversion cycle by business unit?”or“Which customers have receivables aging beyond 60 days?”It brings governed accounts receivable, accounts payable, inventory, and related finance data into one analysis, so users can explore what is driving a trend rather than manually assemble every input and act on it sooner.

For recurring work, an analyst can create a skill for their own workflow or save a conversation as a Genie Agent that the broader team can reuse and refine. A team can then schedule a weekly working-capital review or a daily receivables-aging update, with results posted to a chat thread and sent by email with any visualizations and a PDF attachment.

## Financial close management

The problem:Quarter- and year-end close is a high-pressure period for controllers and accounting teams. They must reconcile balances, resolve intercompany differences, and ensure each entity is ready to report by a fixed deadline. While dashboards show progress and open tasks, teams still need to investigate the transactions behind exceptions, especially unresolved reconciliations that could affect reported results.

How Genie One helps:Genie One helps accounting teams move from a close exception to the underlying transactions. A corporate controller can ask:“Which entities have unreconciled intercompany balances above $100,000?”or“Show manual journal entries posted during the final three business days of the quarter, ranked by amount.”

Genie One respects each user’s existing permissions, so the controller can prioritize the largest unresolved items across the enterprise to keep the close on track, while a regional accountant automatically sees only the entities and transactions relevant to her role. She can run the same analysis as the corporate controller, scoped to her close calendar and transactions, without anyone configuring a separate vieworduplicating work.

## Spend analytics

The problem:When spend rises, procurement and finance leaders need to decide whether to renegotiate, enforce contract compliance, consolidate suppliers, or accept the change as necessary. The challenge is distinguishing normal business growth from avoidable cost increases, such as price increases, off-contract buying, or a shift toward higher-cost suppliers. A dashboard can identify where spending changed, but it doesn’t establish which supplier or purchasing behavior should be addressed first.

How Genie One helps:A procurement director can ask:“What percentage of addressable spend is off-contract?”or“Which MRO suppliers increased unit prices by more than 10% year over year?”Genie One helps trace a spend pattern from the aggregate to the relevant suppliers and purchase activity; the director can also bring in relevant company materials such as contract summaries, procurement policies, and supplier documents, to understand whether the spend aligns with negotiated terms and purchasing policy.

That gives procurement and finance a shared, evidence-based view of the issue: whether to prioritize a supplier negotiation, address noncompliant buying, pursue supplier consolidation, or prepare for a cost review before unnecessary spend compounds. The analysis remains grounded in governed business data, while supporting materials add the context needed to interpret the finding rather than replace the system of record.
