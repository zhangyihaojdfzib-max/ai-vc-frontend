## Why the balance sheet became finance's front line

The sell side runs on its balance sheet. As markets move and spreads shift, funding costs, capital consumption, and liquidity demands move with them, each shaping how efficiently the firm can deploy scarce balance sheet and generate returns. Helping the business understand those tradeoffs and protect profitability has long been finance's job.

That job has gotten harder. Volatility is up, trading books turn over faster, and Basel III Endgame together with the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) raise the capital required to support market risk. Industry analyses have warned that the FRTB standardized approach can materially affect market-risk capital requirements, depending on portfolio composition, jurisdiction, and modeling approach. Finance sits at the center of it, translating an increasingly complex set of funding, capital, and liquidity decisions for trading, treasury, and leadership.

Now add AI agents embedded in pricing workflows, funding recommendations, reporting, surveillance, and other processes across the trading book. It isn't only that AI is informing more decisions, its that those decisions now touch funding, capital, liquidity, and valuation at the same time, and the pace of that shift will keep accelerating.

## Why finance needs business context, not just data

That's the job of an ontology: capturing meaning and keeping it current as the business changes. On the sell side, meaning is inseparable from the firm’s own balance sheet—a position isn’t just a number, it's capital consumed, funding drawn, and risk the desk is carrying until it's hedged or unwound.

For capital markets, firms can anchor that context in established standards such as FIBO, the Financial Industry Business Ontology, which defines how desks, products, and instruments relate to one another in terms a risk or finance system can reason over. Paired with the firm’s governed data and business definitions, that context lets finance read a number the same way across front office, treasury, risk, and regulatory reporting.
