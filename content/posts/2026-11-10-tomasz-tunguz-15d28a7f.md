In short :KC, Chief People Officer at OpenAI & former CIO at Palantir, argues that efficiency is the wrong frame for AI. The prize is a reimagined firm where every role gains an agent manager & EQ becomes what separates people.

Last week we welcomedKC, Chief People Officer at OpenAI, to Office Hours to talk about the tremendous change inside organizations.

KC traced four years of model progress in three steps. Two years ago, the parlor trick was asking a model how many R’s are in strawberry. A year ago, reasoning models solved competition-level math. In September, OpenAI’s agents attacked the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem & said they had a solution in 88 hours1.

That implies a choice for companies : accomplish the same tasks faster or reimagine what is possible. The first lifts earnings & profit. The second bends the arc of a business. At first the slopes look the same. Then the geometric compounding creates explosive separation.

In this future, the people team needs to define how we work.

First, in answer to the most common questions from attendees (how does one evaluate junior talent?) KC replied the words junior & senior lack meaning today.

“I think it’s incorrect to use the word junior and senior. I think we should completely get rid of that word, because for the world that we’re heading to, junior usually means that you don’t have experience that’s relevant for a particular context. But for the world we’re heading to, maybe all of us don’t have experience that’s relevant for that particular context.”

What one knows about their work as of two years ago matters less than what you knew three months ago, so experience fades fast.

Next, he drew his vision of modern work. The model is a brain. Around it sits the harness, Codex or Claude Code, which he called an exoskeleton : skills, tools, & a runtime that holds context across agents. Above that sit incarnations : an assistant, then specialists.

Most firms use people as middleware for their own systems. Do the work, then log the work. He calls it work on work.

“The moment you relegate the system updating to the agents, humans start working with humans.”

That reframes each of our titles : add a suffix : agent manager. I am not a salesperson but a sales agent manager.

The session closed on EQ (emotional intelligence), through a story about an oncologist’s funeral. A patient saw several doctors with the same diagnosis & plan. She picked this one for his bedside manner.

“The differentiator is how you show up as a human. It’s how you show up, how you act with integrity, how you are really humble and want to learn.”

When everyone can reach for intelligence beyond their own, that is what still separates us. KC thinks that is good news. It sends us back to work worth doing.

I’m grateful to KC for sharing his insights at Office Hours!
