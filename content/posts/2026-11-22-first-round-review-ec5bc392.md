When Adit Abraham is at the grocery store, he’s thinking about ideas for his company Reducto. When he goes to bed, he’s thinking about something related to Reducto. When he wakes up, he immediately picks up the Reducto idea he fell asleep thinking about.

Applied Compute’s Yash Patil falls asleep with his laptop on his belly, lights still on. He actually finds it uncomfortable to step away from work.

Gumloop’s Max Brodeur-Urbas works until around 1 AM at the office, and then he takes a Waymo home, walks in the door, brushes his teeth, and lies down, and within five seconds he’s asleep.

But these founders were all quick to clarify that while they're not mandating around-the-clock office presence, there are few moments in their days that aren’t consumed by their companies. They feel anxiety when they’re not working. As AI makes everyone move faster, including your competition, stillness becomes unsettling.

“You can’t take a half-step toward building a startup. It’s less about the hours, and more about how all-consuming it becomes,” says Adit Abraham. “The company becomes an extension of your identity. I can’t separate Reducto from me. You can call that unhealthy.”
